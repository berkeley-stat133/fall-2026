Concept Acquisition

  1. Area-weighted interpolation moves values from source polygons to target polygons in proportion to overlapping area, assuming each value is spread evenly over its source.
  2. Counts like votes are spatially extensive (split with area); rates and shares are intensive (averaged).
  3. SV precincts are drawn by counties each election and may not match a geographic area; SR precincts are stable statewide units with both vote counts and shapefiles.
  4. The project’s new-map votes can be estimated by area (Approach A) or through census blocks weighted by registered voters (Approach B).

Tool Acquisition

  2. st_intersection() to cut one layer by another.

Spatial Interpolation

Area-weighted Interpolation

Estimates a value for each target polygon by giving it, from each source polygon it overlaps, the share of the source’s value equal to the share of the source’s area that falls inside the target. Assumes each value is spread evenly over its source.

st_interpolate_aw(x, to, extensive)

library(sf)

    st_polygon(list(rbind(c(0,0), c(.5,0), c(.5,.5), c(0,1), c(0,0)))),

By hand

library(dplyr)
pieces <- precincts_sf |>
  mutate(precinct_area = st_area(geometry)) |>
  st_intersection(districts_sf) |>
  mutate(piece_area = st_area(geometry),
         piece_votes = votes * as.numeric(piece_area / precinct_area))

st_intersection() cuts one layer by another: each piece keeps the columns of both.

Guess the Metaphor

Spatial interpolation is like a tea valley: when a new path cuts through the bushes (a new district line through the precincts), the harvest on each side is its share of every bush it crosses, as long as the leaves grow evenly over each bush (votes are spread evenly over each precinct).

A tale of two precinct types

SV Precincts

  1. Created by counties each election
  2. Two counties might use the same precinct name
  3. Unique ID: SVPREC x FIPS
  4. May not correspond to geographic area Example:
    • SVPREC: 30010 holds the vote by mail
    • SVPREC: 30010A holds the in-person votes

    This is why we won’t have shapefiles for every precinct

A tale of two precinct types

SR Precincts

  1. Created by the statewide data base to have a stable precinct unit from election to election.
  2. One SR precinct is generally an aggregation of smaller SV precincts.

We can get both votes and shapefiles for SR precincts.
